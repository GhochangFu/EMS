01

Every gigabyte below is derived from bytes per row measured on the pilot database on 2026-08-10, not from an estimate of rows per day. Bytes per row is independent of scan rate, so it survives a change to the assumption above; rows per day does not.

One correction to the ADRs, and it matters for the long horizon. ADR 0023 extrapolates _1h at ≈35 MB/year and _1d at ≈22 MB/year from measurements of 480 kB and 304 kB. Those figures imply about 16 bytes per row, which is below a bare PostgreSQL row-store tuple header — the tables were small enough that page granularity dominated the reading. This plan uses ~200 B/row instead, so its _1h/_1d numbers are roughly 13× the ADR's. Those are exactly the two levels that are never dropped and never compressed, so understating them is the error that surfaces in year five rather than year two. Worth a correction to ADR 0023.

02

Three findings that change the plan

These came out of the sizing work rather than going into it. Each one changes a decision someone would otherwise make by default.

FINDING 01 Amazon RDS cannot run this database

timescaledb does not appear in the supported-extension list for Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL 16 — verified against the AWS documentation on 2026-08-30. Aurora PostgreSQL does not offer it either.

Even where a managed PostgreSQL service does expose TimescaleDB, it is normally the Apache-2 edition, which gives hypertables and time_bucket but not add_compression_policy, add_retention_policy or add_continuous_aggregate_policy. Migrations 0027 and 0028 are built on all three. An Apache-2 build would take the platform's 29× compression and its entire rollup ladder off the table.

So the database tier in every option below is self-managed PostgreSQL 16 + TimescaleDB on EC2 with EBS. Tiger Cloud (managed TimescaleDB, on AWS, available through AWS Marketplace with VPC peering) is the credible managed alternative and is priced as an option in section 06. Everything else in the stack — Redis, Keycloak's own database, object storage — can and should use the managed AWS service.

FINDING 02 At a 60-second scan the 1-minute rollup costs more than raw

The rollup levels are sized by tag count, not by scan rate: 500 tags produce 720,000 _1m rows a day whether the sensors are read every second or every minute. Raw is sized by both. So as the scan interval lengthens, raw shrinks and the rollups do not.

Scan intervalRaw rows per 1m bucket Raw, per org_1m, per org _1m as % of raw
5 s12.0070.8 GB 10.7 GB15% ladder pays
15 s base4.00 23.6 GB10.7 GB45% watch
60 s1.005.9 GB 10.7 GB181% inverted

At 60 s the _1m aggregate stores one bucket per raw sample — it costs nearly twice what raw costs and adds no resolution at all. The ladder ADR 0023 chose is efficient at the 1 Hz target it was designed against and inefficient at a slow scan. If Ion Exchange settles on a 60-second scan, dropping _1m from the ladder is worth an ADR. That is a scope decision, not a sizing one.

FINDING 03 Two levels never expire, so one line on this plan has no ceiling

_1h and _1d are never dropped and never compressed. They add about 0.85 GB per organization per year, forever — 8.5 GB/year at 10 orgs, 85 GB/year at 100. That is small against a 4.3 TB fleet and it is the only line here that does not reach a steady state, so it belongs in the five-year budget rather than the two-year one. Nothing needs to change today; it needs a review date.

06

What this plan does not include

Stated so that nobody discovers them after the budget is approved. Several are cheap; one is not.

ExcludedRough monthly impactNote
Disaster recovery to a second region +35–60% Cross-region replica plus snapshot copy. The single largest omission. Decide whether the RPO justifies it before sizing.
Non-production environments +15–25% Dev and staging at roughly Tier A shape, run on a schedule rather than 24/7.
AWS Business Support +3–10% Tiered on spend. Effectively mandatory for a production platform with an SLA.
Tiger Cloud instead of self-managed quote required Managed TimescaleDB. Trades cost for the operational burden of running Postgres — patching, failover, backup verification, major-version upgrades. It costs more than the EC2 line it replaces; how much more is a vendor quote, not an estimate this document should invent. Worth pricing properly against one engineer's time.
Site-to-cloud connectivity varies VPN or Direct Connect from each plant. Belongs to the client's network budget, not this one.
Licences and third-party services varies EMQX Enterprise if the open-source edition is outgrown; SMS and email gateways for notifications; certificate management.
Security tooling +5–8% GuardDuty, Security Hub, WAF, secrets rotation. Assume it is required, not optional, for a platform holding customer plant data.
07

Where this design stops scaling

Tier D is the last point the current architecture reaches by growing the instance. Past roughly 100 organizations at this scan rate, something has to change — and the choice is a decision for the repo owner, not for a sizing document.

The constraint is not insert throughput. 3,333 rows per second is comfortable for TimescaleDB. The constraint is that one writer carries every tenant's ingest, four levels of continuous-aggregate refresh, the compression job, and every dashboard and report query at once. Three specific pressures arrive together:

PressureWhat it looks like at 100 orgs
Aggregate refresh work The _1m level materialises 72 million rows a day. The refresh is hierarchical and cheap per level, but the base level is not.
Compression window 288 million rows a day pass the 7-day boundary daily. Compression rewrites whole chunks and wants both IO and a quiet period to do it in.
Migration locks ADR 0024 measured ALTER TABLE … SET (timescaledb.compress …) taking an ACCESS EXCLUSIVE lock on the telemetry table. The work is 10.7 ms; the wait is not. Every schema change is a fleet-wide event.

Grow vertically

r7i.12xlarge or r7i.16xlarge, or the memory-dense x2iedn family. Buys perhaps another 2×.

CHEAPEST · NO CODE CHANGE · CEILING MOVES, DOES NOT LIFT

Split telemetry onto its own cluster

Move the telemetry schema to a dedicated TimescaleDB cluster and leave bms.* on the OLTP cluster. The API already holds separate pools per database role.

SPLITS BY TABLE, NOT BY TENANT · NEEDS AN ADR

Move to Tiger Cloud

Managed TimescaleDB on AWS, with its own scaling and read-replica story. Costs more and removes the operational burden.

HIGHEST COST · LOWEST OPERATIONAL RISK

The middle option does not reopen ADR 0043, and that distinction matters. ADR 0043 rejected schema-per-tenant and database-per-tenant because both break the single hypertable, the migration runner and every cross-tenant report. Splitting telemetry from OLTP splits by table, not by tenant: the hypertable stays single, row-level security stays the tenant boundary, and no tenant gets its own database. Anything that genuinely reopens ADR 0043 — per-tenant databases, per-tenant retention, a sharded hypertable — is the repo owner's gate under AGENTS.md §10, and this document does not propose it.

08

Decisions this plan is waiting on

Six items. The first four would replace this plan's largest assumptions with facts; the last two are work the sizing exercise itself uncovered, and they belong to Euphoria whatever the client answers.

QuestionOwnerWhat it changes
What scan interval do the plants actually need, per domain? Ion Exchange Storage per organization varies 4.2× across 5 s to 60 s. Electrical trending and effluent compliance may not want the same rate — a per-domain answer is better than one number.
Is 500 tags per organization the average or the ceiling? Ion Exchange Everything here scales linearly in tags. A 500-tag average with a 2,000-tag largest customer is a different plan from a flat 500.
Does any compliance obligation need sample-level effluent data beyond two years? Ion Exchange Already routed to the client in the E5.1 questions. A "yes" removes the 730-day retention on raw and roughly doubles storage per additional year kept.
Self-managed on EC2, or Tiger Cloud? Euphoria A cost-versus-operational-burden call, not a technical one. Both run the same extension; only one of them pages someone at 3 a.m.
If the scan lands at 60 s, does _1m stay in the rollup ladder? Euphoria The one place this exercise produces a change rather than a number. At a 60-second scan _1m costs 181% of raw and adds no resolution (finding 02). Removing a level from the ADR 0023 ladder is a scope decision and needs its own ADR at the AGENTS.md §10 gate.
Correct ADR 0023's _1h / _1d per-year figures Euphoria They read ≈35 MB and ≈22 MB per year, extrapolated from sub-megabyte tables where page granularity dominated; the honest figures are roughly 13× higher (section 01). These are the two levels that are never dropped, so the understatement compounds. A one-paragraph correction, not a re-decision.
