> **For agentic workers:** this plan follows the `superpowers:writing-plans`

**Row:** `F3.16` (P1, Wave 2, not an enabler) in `docs/BACKLOG.md:440`.
**Base:** `main` at `47fbc2ba`. **ADR:** [ADR 0127](../adr/0127-device-health.md).
**Written by** `plan-architect` on Fable, against `main` at `037e0fa8`, then
updated with the owner's rulings. CodeGraph was not available; the blast radius
below is from `grep` and reading, and `tsc` is the enumerator where a column
type changes.

**Goal.** An admin can see, per RTU, when it was last heard, whether it is
Online, Stale or Offline, its clock skew, and how many readings were dropped
and which point was dropped first, with 30 days of hourly history. The footer's
field-data status follows a real ingest-host heartbeat. An RTU that goes silent
raises one "Communication lost" alarm; a silent ingest host raises one "Ingest
host not reporting" alarm per organization and holds the per-RTU ones.

**Out of scope.** `prom-client` or any metrics endpoint. Any change to the
ingest health page. A new rule type. An alarm on clock skew, on drop rate or
on a broker outage. Comms-loss alarms for edge-served RTUs (ADR 0127 decision
18, a new row). Simulator RTUs (decision 20). A dedicated `bms_ingest` role
(still owed, ADR 0045).

## 2. Design in brief (ADR 0127 decisions 7–21)

- **Ingest** attributes every drop to a device inside `resolveSamples`, sums
  per RTU in memory, and flushes every 10 s in one transaction: the host row,
  changed RTU rows, hour buckets.
- **One pure state rule** in the API, `rtuStateAt(row, windowSeconds)`, judged
  against the host's last beat. SQL supplies fields only; JS judges, so the
  four readers (admin page, system status, worker sweep, lifecycle clear)
  cannot disagree.
- **Alarms**: the worker raises through `AlarmRaiser.raiseDevice`; the
  lifecycle sweep clears and escalates through the organization's escalation
  profile.

## 6. Migration notes

- Forward-only. Journal: append `0149_device_health` and `0150_device_alarms`;
  stamp `when` with `Date.now()`, above the previous entry, never hand-pinned
  ahead of the clock.
- Idempotent: `IF NOT EXISTS` on tables, columns and indexes; `DROP POLICY IF
  EXISTS` before `CREATE POLICY`; every `ADD CONSTRAINT` behind a
  `pg_constraint` probe. No statement-breakpoint markers (the `0038` reason).
- `SET ROLE bms_owner` … `RESET ROLE` around the DDL, so `0041`'s default
  privileges reach `bms_tenant` and `bms_fleet`. **Write no `GRANT`.** Inside
  the bracket: `REVOKE INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE` on the two tenant tables and
  `REVOKE ALL` on the two platform tables, `FROM bms_tenant`.
- Tenant tables: `organization_id NOT NULL`, `ENABLE` and `FORCE`,
  `tenant_isolation` with `USING` and `WITH CHECK`, no NULL disjunct.
- A proof `DO` block as the migrator: RLS forced; `bms_tenant` holds `SELECT`
  and no write verb on the tenant tables and nothing on the platform tables;
  `bms_fleet` holds all four verbs; each constraint and index exists by name.
- `SET LOCAL lock_timeout` before touching `bms.rtus` and `bms.alarms`. In
  `0150`, `DROP NOT NULL` and `ADD COLUMN … DEFAULT` are catalog-only; the
  CHECK is `NOT VALID` then `VALIDATE`; the partial index matches no row.
- TimescaleDB: none of the tables is a hypertable and nothing touches
  `telemetry.*`, compression or an aggregate. Volume is at most 24 × 6 rows
  per RTU per day and usually far fewer; the worker's `DELETE` prunes.
